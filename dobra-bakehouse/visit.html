The Saturday first-loaf line

Every Saturday the first bake of rye and country sourdough comes out at 8:00, as the doors open. A line forms on Manhattan Avenue from about 7:40 — neighbors, dogs, a few people who came on the G from Queens. We hand out the first cardamom knots to whoever’s waiting.

Prefer not to queue? A bread subscription holds your loaf on the shelf.

1974

A starter, a grandmother, and a bakery on her old street.

In 1974 Halina Nowak mixed rye flour and water in a jam jar in her kitchen on Nassau Avenue, two blocks from where the bakery stands now. She baked a dark rye every Saturday for forty years and fed the starter every night after dinner.

Her granddaughter Kasia grew up eating that bread, took a jar of the starter to college, and carried it through ten years of baking jobs in Manhattan and Copenhagen. When a storefront came up on Manhattan Avenue in 2017, she brought it home.

“People ask what makes the rye taste like that. Mostly it’s fifty years of being fed by people who were paying attention.”

Kasia Nowak

The bakers

Dobra is two bakers, four early-shift staff and a counter team who know most customers by their usual loaf.

Kasia Nowak

Baker & co-founder · bread

Born on Nassau Avenue to a family from Kraków. Trained in bread at a Copenhagen bakery and ran the bread program at a Tribeca restaurant before opening Dobra. She is in at 3am four days a week, shapes every rye loaf herself and runs the Sunday classes.

Theo Alvarez

Pastry chef & co-founder · sweet

Grew up in Jackson Heights and came to pastry through a Midtown hotel kitchen. Theo’s cardamom knots started as a staff-meal experiment and became the bestseller; his babka uses Kasia’s grandmother’s dough with his own chocolate-cardamom filling. He also fries every pączek.

Dobra Bakehouse

Dobra means “good” in Polish. Bread and pastry baked every open morning on Manhattan Avenue since 2017.
