一The counter

Below East 7th Street, a room that holds its breath.

Eleven steps down from the sidewalk. A single slab of Kiso hinoki, sanded each morning, oiled each night. Ten seats facing the chef, nothing behind you but a paper screen and the hum of the city above.

There is no menu to read. You will be told what each piece is, where it came from, and how long it rested. Then it is yours, for about eight seconds, before the rice cools.

三The chef

Daisuke Arai 新井 大輔

Twenty-two years behind a counter, beginning at sixteen as the boy who washed rice at a six-seat shop in Fukuoka’s Nakasu district. Seven years before he was allowed to touch fish.

He came to New York in 2011 to cook for others. Hōsen, which roughly means “the line drawn with a square,” is the first room that is his: a place where the only straight lines are the counter and the knife.

“The rice is the host. The fish is the guest.”

五Etiquette

A few quiet requests

  1. Arrive on time. All ten guests begin together. We hold seats fifteen minutes, then the first course is served without you.
  2. Eat each piece promptly. With fingers or chopsticks, as you like. Soy has already been applied.
  3. No strong fragrance. It travels along the counter.
  4. Photographs without flash are welcome. Calls are not.
  5. Allergies in advance. Tell us when you book. We cannot accommodate shellfish allergies or a menu without raw fish.
  6. Cancellations 48 hours ahead. Later than that, we charge $150 per seat — the fish was bought for you.
