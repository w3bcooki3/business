Breakfast in a greenhouse, on Manhattan Avenue.

A café, matcha bar and plant shop under the original skylights of a 1920s florist. Eggs until close, whisked tea, and something green to carry home.

Fig. — Ficus lyrata, 1924 skylight, east bay

Aggie grows them. Sam bakes. You stay as long as you like.

Agnieszka Nowak’s great-grandfather sold carnations from this storefront for forty years. When the lease came up in 2020, she — a horticulturist who’d spent a decade at a botanic garden — and her partner Sam Okafor, a pastry chef tired of 4 am wake-ups, decided to bring the glass back.

They stripped the drop ceiling to find the skylights intact. Now there are 300 plants in the room, about half of them for sale, and a kitchen that serves breakfast from opening to close because, as Sam puts it, “nobody in Greenpoint wakes up at the same time.”

The matcha bar

We buy from one family farm in Uji, Kyoto, and whisk every bowl by hand with a bamboo chasen. It’s green because it’s fresh: matcha loses colour, and sweetness, within weeks of opening, so we only open tins we’ll finish in ten days.

  1. Sift2 g through a fine mesh
  2. Pour70 ml at 80 °C
  3. WhiskIn a W, fifteen seconds

Tins to take home, 30 g, $28. Whisks and bowls in the shop.

The plant shop

Every plant leaves with a care card, written by Aggie, and a promise: if it struggles in the first month, bring it back and we’ll nurse it.

  • Light Bright, indirect. Can take a little morning sun.

    Water When the top 5 cm are dry. About weekly.

    Good for People who want drama without much effort.

  • Light Bright, indirect. Rotate weekly so it grows straight.

    Water Let it dry halfway. Leaves droop when thirsty.

    Good for Sharing — it makes babies. Bring them to the swap.

  • Light As much bright light as you can give. South or west window.

    Water Deeply, then wait until dry. Hates being moved.

    Good for Patient people with good windows.

  • Light Anything. Even a north-facing bathroom.

    Water Every three weeks. Less in winter.

    Good for First plants, travelers, the forgetful.

  • Light Medium, indirect. Direct sun burns the stripes.

    Water Keep lightly moist; use filtered water if leaves brown.

    Good for Humid apartments — radiator rooms need a pebble tray.

  • Light Low to bright. Variegation fades in dim corners.

    Water When the top half is dry.

    Good for Bookshelves and trailing off the fridge.

Pets: Monstera, pothos and snake plant are toxic to cats and dogs if chewed. Pilea and calathea are pet-safe. Ask us — we’ll steer you right.
