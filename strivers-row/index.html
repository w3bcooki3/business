Editor’s letter

From the owner

“My grandfather got his hair cut on 125th every Saturday for fifty years. He said a barbershop is where a neighborhood holds its meetings. We cut hair. We also hold the meetings.”

— Marcus Bell, owner since 2016

Strivers Row Barbers sits on Frederick Douglass Boulevard at 128th, ten blocks from the row houses that gave us the name. Six chairs, a wall that changes artists every season, and a standard that doesn’t change at all: you leave looking like the best version of yourself.

Walk-in board

Two chairs every day are kept for walk-ins. Sign the clipboard by the door, grab a seat, and we’ll call your name. Typical waits:

  • Tue–Thu 10–20 minutes
  • Friday after 4 30–45 minutes
  • Saturday doors at 8, list is full by 11
  • Sunday appointments only

Estimates, not promises. Text and we’ll tell you how it looks right now.

04

The Block

Back-to-school cuts

Every year on the last Saturday of August, the shop opens at 7 and every kid with a backpack gets a free cut. Last year: 212 heads. Volunteers from three barbershops uptown help us keep up.

The wall

Each season, a Harlem artist takes over the long wall. This fall: large-scale linocut portraits of Strivers’ Row residents, 1919–1940. Prints for sale, all proceeds to the artist.

Why the name

The townhouses on 138th and 139th Streets were nicknamed Strivers’ Row for the doctors, musicians and lawyers who lived there. Marcus’s grandfather delivered their mail. We aim to keep the standard.
