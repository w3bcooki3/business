Twoneighbors,one window

Desmond Clarke roasted coffee for other people for eleven years. Mireille Joseph lived two doors down and baked her grandmother's pain patate for every block party on Halsey. In 2021, the garden level of number 387 came up for rent, and the window faced the sidewalk at exactly cup height.

So that's the whole shop: a counter, a machine, a pastry case and a window that opens onto the stoop. There are no tables. There's the stoop, and there's yours. Most mornings the steps fill with strollers, dog walkers, and someone's uncle who knows everybody.

Meet Desmond and Mireille

"My grandmother made pain patate in Jacmel with whatever sweet potatoes looked best that week. I do the same thing at the Fulton Street market."

Mireille Joseph, co-owner and baker

Life onthe stoop

A few things that happen on our steps that don't fit on the menu board.

  • Dogs drink free

    The blue bowl by the railing gets filled every hour, and there's a jar of peanut-butter biscuits behind the counter. Ask, and say their name.

  • Beans by the bag

    Brownstone or the current single origin, whole bean or ground for your setup. $19 for 12 oz, roasted within the week.

  • The plants are the neighbors'

    Half the pots on the steps belong to people on the block who travel. We water them; they get a free latte when they're back.

The stoopcard

No app, no account, no email. A paper card we punch at the window. Nine coffees, and the tenth is on us: any drink, any size.

Lost it? Tell us your name. We keep a shoebox of spares for regulars, and Desmond remembers more of you than you'd think.

A swap, not a sale

No money changes hands except at the coffee window. Neighbors set up on their own steps from Throop to Marcy: books, records, kids' clothes, the bread machine nobody used. We bring out a folding table for anything free and keep the cold brew coming.
