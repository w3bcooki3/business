Neighborsfirst,café second

Stoop Coffee is two people, one espresso machine and a window cut into the garden level of a Halsey Street brownstone. Here's how it happened.

Desmond and Mireille

Desmond Clarke

Co-owner, coffee

Desmond spent eleven years roasting for wholesale outfits in Red Hook and Bushwick, dialing in blends for cafés he never got to stand behind. Born in Crown Heights to Jamaican parents, he moved to Halsey Street in 2016 and noticed the block had a dozen stoops and nowhere to get a decent cup within six minutes.

He pulls every shot of the morning rush himself and still cups each new batch of Brownstone before it goes in the hopper.

Mireille Joseph

Co-owner, kitchen

Mireille grew up between Flatbush and her grandmother's kitchen in Jacmel, where pain patate was made on Sundays with whatever sweet potatoes were best at the market. She worked as a school nurse for twelve years and baked for every block party on Halsey until the neighbors started asking where to buy it.

Every bake on the board is hers: some straight from her grandmother, some (the pikliz toast) a little more Brooklyn.

How thewindowopened

  1. 2016

    Desmond moves onto Halsey. Mireille, two doors down, brings over a pain patate as a welcome. He brings coffee back. A habit starts.

  2. 2020

    Summer on the stoops. Neighbors meet on their steps every morning, six feet apart, and Desmond's thermos becomes the block's coffee supply.

  3. 2021

    The garden level at 387 comes up for rent. The landlord, Mrs. Beatrice Holloway, lets them cut a service window under the stoop. Stoop Coffee opens in May.

  4. Now

    Five years, one punch-card shoebox, roughly 200 cups a morning, and a monthly stoop sale that runs from Throop to Marcy.

Theroaster

We don't roast on site; the garden level would be a smoke box. Our beans come from a small-batch partner roaster a few neighborhoods over, on a 15-kilo machine Desmond used to run.

We roast Brownstone weekly and order the single origin in small lots, so it changes every couple of months. Right now it's a washed Ethiopia from the Guji zone: peach, jasmine, a little black tea at the end. Every bag on the shelf is dated, and nothing sits longer than three weeks.

Ask Desmond what's next. He'll tell you more than you asked.

Theblock

Halsey between Tompkins and Throop is a row of 1890s brownstones with stoops that are used every single day: for coffee, for gossip, for watching the kids ride bikes in circles.

We think of the café as the block's front porch. Dogs get water and a biscuit. Plants on the steps belong to whoever brought them. Kids who show a report card get a free hot chocolate in June. If you're new, sit down on a step and somebody will tell you which bodega has the best chopped cheese.
