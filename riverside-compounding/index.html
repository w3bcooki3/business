§1 — Catalogue

When the shelf version doesn’t fit.

Compounding means preparing a medicine for one person from a prescriber’s order: a different form, a different base, without an ingredient you can’t take. Six things we’re asked for most.

  1. No. 01

    Allergen- & dye-free

    Prepared without lactose, gluten, dyes, sugar or a preservative you react to — whenever the formula allows it.

    Ask about: lactose · gluten · dyes · sugar · alcohol

  2. No. 02

    Liquids & other forms

    For anyone who can’t swallow a tablet: suspensions, solutions, lozenges, troches and topical gels, as your prescriber specifies.

    Forms: liquid · lozenge · troche · gel · capsule

  3. No. 03

    Flavored for children

    A taste your child will actually take — chosen together at the counter from our flavor shelf, cherry to bubblegum.

    Flavors chosen in person

  4. No. 04

    Veterinary

    Your vet’s prescription, in a form your pet accepts: flavored liquids, chewable treats, or a transdermal gel for cats who won’t be pilled.

    Flavors: chicken · fish · beef

  5. No. 05

    Hormone prescriptions

    Creams, capsules and troches prepared exactly as your physician writes them, with a pharmacist available to answer questions.

    By prescription only

  6. No. 06

    Dermatology

    Custom creams, ointments and solutions from your dermatologist’s formula — in the base and strength they specify.

    Cream · ointment · solution · gel

Note. Compounded preparations are made for an individual patient from a valid prescription. They are not FDA-approved products; your prescriber and our pharmacist decide together whether compounding is right. Compounds are often not covered by insurance — we quote the price before we begin.

§2 — Method

How a prescription is compounded.

Six steps, recorded on a worksheet for every batch. Most preparations are ready in one to two business days — we text you when yours is.

  1. 01 · Prescription

    It begins with your prescriber.

    Your doctor, dermatologist or veterinarian sends the order by e-prescription or fax — or you bring the paper prescription to the counter.

  2. 02 · Verify

    A pharmacist reviews the formula.

    Ingredients, strength, compatibility, the allergies on your profile and the right base for the form you need. Any question goes back to the prescriber before anything is weighed.

  3. 03 · Weigh

    Every ingredient, on the balance.

    Each component is weighed on a calibrated balance and written onto the compounding worksheet for that batch — nothing by eye.

  4. 04 · Make

    Prepared by hand.

    In our compounding room, following the recorded formula: triturated, mixed, suspended or encapsulated — depending on what was prescribed.

  5. 05 · Check

    A second set of eyes.

    Weights, appearance and the worksheet are reviewed by a pharmacist before anything is released.

  6. 06 · Label

    Labeled, and ready.

    Directions exactly as your prescriber wrote them, a beyond-use date and storage instructions. We text you; pick up at the counter or ask about local delivery.
