Compounding
Medicine made for one person at a time.

        
When the right dose, form or flavor doesn’t exist on a manufacturer’s shelf, our pharmacists prepare it by hand from your doctor’s prescription, in the back room Erik Halvorsen built in 1948.

      

      
    
        
  • ChildrenLiquids they’ll actually take
    Grape, bubblegum or watermelon. Dye-free on request.
    • 
        
  • SkinDermatology creams
    The strength your dermatologist specifies, in fragrance-free bases.
    • 
        
  • AllergiesWithout the fillers
    Remade without lactose, gluten or dyes where your prescriber agrees.
    • 
        
  • PetsVeterinary prescriptions
    Flavored liquids and chewables. Chicken is the favorite on 81st Street.
    • 
      

      
Compounded preparations are made to an individual prescription. Ask us whether your plan covers them.

Medicare help

        
Is your Part D plan still the right one?

        
Plans change their prices and covered drugs every year. Bring your medicine list (or let us print it) and a pharmacist will compare plans with you on Medicare’s own Plan Finder. Free, about twenty minutes, no sales pitch.

Services

      
Everything a neighborhood pharmacy should do.

      

        
Prescriptions
Most commercial plans, Medicare Part D and NY Medicaid. We call your doctor for renewals so you don’t have to.

        
Vaccines
Flu, COVID-19, shingles, pneumonia, RSV and Tdap. Walk in weekdays 9–6, or call for a time.

        
Blister packs
Every dose sorted into dated, sealed cards. Free for anyone taking four or more medicines.

        
Medication reviews
Thirty unhurried minutes going through everything you take, supplements included. No charge.

        
Home medical supplies
Canes, compression stockings, blood-pressure monitors and post-surgery supplies, fitted properly.

        
For caregivers
Picking up for a parent? We keep a caregiver on file so you can speak for them when needed.

      

    

  


  
  

    

      
Since 1921

      
Four generations, one counter.

      
    
        
  1. 1921
    Anders Halvorsen, a Norwegian-trained chemist, opens a drugstore and soda fountain at Lexington and 81st.
    2. 
        
  2. 1948
    His son Erik adds a compounding room in the back. We still use it.
    3. 
        
  3. 1976
    Karin Halvorsen becomes one of the first women to own a pharmacy on the Upper East Side.
    4. 
        
  4. 2009
    Bicycle delivery begins with one courier, Desmond, who still rides for us.
    5. 
        
  5. Today
    Dr. Ingrid Halvorsen leads a team of five pharmacists.
