On this corner since 1911

One family, one corner, five generations

The pharmacy has had new floors, new computers and a new awning or two. It still has a Hartley behind the counter, and it still knows your name.

Our story, briefly

Ruth likes to say the pharmacy has outlasted three subway lines’ worth of construction and every chain that opened on Seventh Avenue. Mostly it’s outlasted because the neighborhood kept coming back.

  1. 1911Erik Hartley, a pharmacist from Bergen, Norway, opens a drugstore at Bleecker and Grove. He sells cough syrup, mixes prescriptions and rents the upstairs to a printer.
  2. 1946His son Walter comes home from the Navy and takes over. He starts delivering by bicycle, which we still do.
  3. 1979Walter’s daughter Ingrid earns her pharmacy degree and becomes the first woman to run the store. During the AIDS crisis, she becomes known for staying open late, delivering to anyone who was too sick to come in, and treating every patient with dignity.
  4. 1998Ruth Hartley-Diaz, Ingrid’s daughter, joins the counter, and so does Sam Weintraub.
  5. 2020During the pandemic, the team delivers to every corner of the Village and gives more than 6,000 COVID shots.
  6. TodayRuth runs the pharmacy with her daughter Nora, the fifth generation, and a team that feels like family.
