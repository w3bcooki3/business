Medicaid plan help

Fidelis Care, Healthfirst and MetroPlusHealth each have their own rules. Daniel Reyes sorts them out with you, in English or Spanish:

  • Finding a covered alternative when a medicine is denied
  • Working with your doctor on prior authorizations
  • Explaining NYRx, the New York Medicaid pharmacy program

Bring your plan card and the letter if you got one. It usually takes one visit.

Refills and transfers

Refill a prescription

  1. Call or text (718) 555-0187 with your name, date of birth and the Rx number from your bottle label.
  2. We fill it and check your insurance. If anything needs your doctor, we text you.
  3. Pick it up, or get it delivered free in Belmont, Fordham and the Bronx Park area.

Transfer to Loreto

Call us with your old pharmacy’s name and the medicines you take. We contact them, and your doctor if refills ran out, and call you when it’s ready, usually within one business day. Transfers are free.

Three generations on Arthur Avenue

Salvatore Loreto opened the pharmacy at 2381 Arthur Avenue in 1961. His son Paolo took over the counter in the eighties, and his granddaughter Teresa runs it today. The cabinets are older than most of our customers; the systems are not. Every prescription is checked twice, plans are billed electronically, refills go out by text, and you still get a pharmacist who knows your name.

Questions people ask us

Something else? Call (718) 555-0187. A person answers.

Do you deliver?

Yes, free. Same day to Belmont, Fordham and the Bronx Park area for orders in by 2 pm. Outside that area, call and we’ll see what we can do.

Can I transfer my prescriptions to you?

Yes. Call with your old pharmacy’s name and what you take. We contact them and your doctor and call you when it’s ready, usually within one business day.

Do you take my insurance?

We take most plans, including NY Medicaid, Fidelis Care, Healthfirst, MetroPlusHealth, Medicare Part D and EPIC. See the full list or call to check yours.

Do I need an appointment for a flu shot?

No. Walk in any day we’re open, Sundays too. Bring your insurance card. For travel vaccines, call ahead so we have yours in stock.

How long does a new prescription take?

Most are ready the same day, often within the hour. If we need to order a medicine or check with your insurance, we’ll tell you when to expect it.

Do you have lower prices if I pay cash?

Yes, on many common generics, and we accept discount cards. Call with the name of your medicine and we’ll give you a price.

Can you put my medicines in pill packs?

Yes, free. We sort your medicines by day and time into easy-open packs and can deliver them with your refills each month.
